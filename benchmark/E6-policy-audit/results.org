* What we measure, and why

A policy auditor answers one question continuously: /which resources
violate which policies, right now?/ Gatekeeper's audit answers it by
brute force - every ~--audit-interval~ (default 60 s) it re-fetches the
inventory and re-evaluates every constraint against every resource.
$\Delta$-policy answers it incrementally: the same stock Gatekeeper policy
objects, the same Rego, but evaluated as deltas, so a violation appears
when the violating change happens and disappears when it is reverted.

Both contenders run as their shipped binaries against the same envtest
plant, audited by one instrument: the driver's clock, a watch on the
constraint statuses, and a content predicate on ~totalViolations~ - 
identical for every mode. Modes: ~dpolicy~ (reconciled), ~dpolicy-smith~
(the Smith dead-time compensator instead of the Reconciler: exactly-once
status actuation, echoes retired by content), ~gk~ (stock default:
per-cycle re-LIST), ~gk-cache~ (~--audit-from-cache~, the
architecture-isolating ablation: fetch cost gone, periodic re-evaluation
remains).

* Method notes

- One fresh envtest plant per (mode, N, P) grid point; the contender
  runs out of process (the stock gatekeeper binary with
  ~audit+status+generate~ operations, or the $\Delta$-policy controller), the
  driver hosts none of them.
- Client budgets as shipped: Gatekeeper configures no client-side rate
  limit (client-go's 5 QPS / burst 10 - at P=50 a single cycle's status
  writes alone spend seconds inside its own limiter); $\Delta$-policy's runtime
  defaults to 50/100. The driver loads at 500/1000 through sharded
  updaters, identically for every mode.
- The corpus is stock Gatekeeper policy YAML, byte-identical for both
  engines ($\Delta$-policy evaluates the same Rego through the embedded OPA).
- W2's API-request counts come from the plant's own request-audit log,
  attributed by user agent; CPU/RSS from one /proc sampler over the
  contender's process.
- The audit interval is normalized to 10 s: a periodic scanner's
  detection behavior is scale-free in $L/\text{interval}$ (W3) and
  latency/interval (W1), so the compressed interval measures the same
  curves in a fraction of the wall clock; Gatekeeper's as-deployed
  default is 60 s, and the numbers scale linearly.
- W3's reps run concurrently, one probe namespace + probe constraint per
  rep (independent 0/1 counts), creations staggered across one audit
  interval - uniform phase sampling where sequential reps would
  phase-lock with the scanner's cycle (and Σ(L)·reps of wall clock
  becomes Σ(L)).
