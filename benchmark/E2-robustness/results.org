* What we measure, and why

Speed is easy to sell; robustness needs a number. Ours is *drift*: the
Z-set distance $\lVert Y_U - D \rVert$ between the plant content and the
desired state (sum of absolute weights: a missing object counts 1, a
spurious object 1, a corrupted object 2). The desired state is known
analytically - the fixtures are synthetic, so an oracle computes the
correct endpoints documents from the current inputs in closed form.

The controller is the pair-case endpoints pipeline (N services, 1:1
pods). Every round: one pod churns (the background workload that keeps
each architecture doing work), the controller reacts, then a seeded
noise source tampers with the plant behind the controller's back - 
deleting a random object (a lost or reverted write), corrupting one (an
out-of-band edit), or inserting a spurious one (an unmanaged leftover) - 
at rate $\lambda$ disturbances/round. Drift is sampled at the end of
every round.

#+begin_example
              +-----------------------+  deltas/snapshots/corrections
   churn ---> |  endpoints controller | -------> +---------+
   (pods)     +-----------------------+  apply   |  plant  | <-- noise W
                          ^                      +---------+    (delete/
                          |        observed (watch)   |          corrupt/
                          +--------- reconciled only -+          insert)
                                                       drift = |plant - oracle|
#+end_example

The disturbance is /reported/ to the controller only in reconciled mode
(that is the point of the watch); SotW never sees it but overwrites
everything on its next emission; the open-loop incremental controller
never sees it and never overwrites.

Predictions from the loop model:

- *SotW*: drift is wiped by the next full write - a sawtooth bounded by
  one round's noise, mean $\approx \lambda \cdot \text{(sampling offset)}$,
  never accumulating.
- *incremental (open loop)*: every disturbance in the blind spot stays;
  drift grows without bound, slope $\propto \lambda$ (minus the
  disturbances that later churn happens to overwrite).
- *reconciled*: the watch report triggers a correction the same round - 
  the deadbeat loop rejects the disturbance in one step; sampled drift
  stays 0.

* Notes on fairness

- The open-loop applier is not strawmanned: it content-checks
  retractions (precondition-guarded deletes, as a careful edge-triggered
  applier would), and later churn on a disturbed object incidentally
  heals it, since inserts write by key. The residual drift comes from
  spurious objects (never retracted) and disturbed objects the workload
  does not happen to touch - which is exactly the real-world failure
  mode.
- SotW's healing is bounded by its emission period. Here it emits on
  every churn event; a SotW controller with a slower resync period
  drifts for correspondingly longer.
- The reconciled mode pays for its robustness with the feedback
  traffic: one observed-topic report per plant change and one (empty)
  confirmation step per correction. E1's update-size figure shows this
  cost is delta-sized, and zero at quiescence.

* Reading the numbers

Fast-profile sweep (N=100, 300 rounds, seed 42).

- *Steady state.* Open-loop drift is linear in the noise rate for
  small $\lambda$ (mean 4.6 / 11.3 / 23.7 at $\lambda$ = 0.05 / 0.1 /
  0.2) and saturating toward the state size at $\lambda = 1$ (112.6
  with only 100 managed objects): every uncorrected disturbance is
  permanent, discounted only by churn incidentally rewriting a
  disturbed key. SotW's mean drift is two orders of magnitude lower
  (0.07-1.39) and /independent of history/ - it is just the noise that
  landed since the last write, never more than one round's worth
  (max 2 across all rates). The reconciled loop sampled 0.00 at every
  rate including one disturbance per round: the correction lands
  within the round, so the sampler never sees residual drift.
- *Burst.* Flat zero until the burst; then the three architectures
  separate exactly as the model predicts: open-loop jumps to 23 and
  stays there for the remaining 50 rounds (MTTR ∞), SotW carries the
  drift for one round and wipes it at its next emission (MTTR 1),
  the reconciled loop absorbs all 20 disturbances within the burst
  round itself (MTTR 0, max drift 0 at sampling) - the deadbeat
  property, measured.
- Robustness here costs what E1 measured: ~30 us and one feedback
  report per plant change. The drift-vs-rate figure is the value side
  of that trade; together they are the paper's claim in two charts:
  delta-priced compute /and/ SotW-grade (better, one-step) healing.
