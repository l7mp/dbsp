* What we measure, and why

E2 studies /drift/ under a disturbed plant. E3 studies the dual cost - 
/actuation/ - under a delayed plant. A reconciliation loop never confirms
a write the instant it lands: the apiserver admits it, a watch re-lists
it, the event propagates back. That round trip is a *dead time* of $dt$
rounds between emitting a correction and observing its effect.

The desired-state Reconciler is deadbeat but memoryless about what it has
already sent: it re-emits the /whole/ outstanding correction $U=\int(\delta
D-\delta Y_U)$ on every step until the watch echo confirms it. Under a dead
time of $dt$ steps the same logical change is therefore actuated many
times over before the loop learns it succeeded. The dual-rate Smith
holds every emitted correction in a window of $K$ ticks of a clock the
driver pulses once per round, and retires the echo against the window:
a window that covers the dead time ($K \ge dt$) actuates every
correction exactly once, a shorter one releases corrections before their
echo and re-emits them, and the plain Reconciler is the window of zero.
The sweep below fixes the plant at $dt = 4$ and walks the window $K$
from $0$ (the Reconciler) past the first window that absorbs the dead
time.

The controller is the pair-case endpoints pipeline (N services, 1:1 pods).
Every round one pod IP churns (the background workload), the loop reacts,
and the plant's feedback clock advances one tick - releasing the echoes
whose dead time has expired. We count the *actuation volume*: entries the
plant applier receives, each one a would-be apiserver write.

#+begin_example
   churn --> +------------+  U (corrections) --> +---------+
   (pods)    | controller |                      |  plant  |
             +------------+ <-- observed, ------- +---------+
                             delayed dt rounds (echo of applied writes)
#+end_example

Everything is synthetic and in-process: the dead time is injected by the
plant, so there is no apiserver, no envtest, no Kubernetes.

Actuation falls monotonically as the window approaches the dead time:
a correction released before its echo is re-emitted, about $\lceil dt /
K \rceil$ times over the dead time, from the Reconciler's full $O(dt)$
at $K = 0$ down to one write per correction from $K = dt$ on. A longer
window ($K > dt$) buys nothing more here; its price is in the next
figure.

** Recovery under write loss

The flip side of the window: every correction entry is dropped with
probability $p = 0.1$ (actuated but lost), and each round measures the
ticks until the churned state lands in the plant. The Reconciler
re-emits the outstanding correction on every step, so a lost write is
re-actuated immediately; the smith loop re-asserts a correction only
when the window releases it unconfirmed - recovery latency grows with
the window, the price of the silence the amplification sweep buys. The median is 0 for every loop (at $p = 0.1$
nine writes in ten land at once); the tail carries the story, so the
figure plots p95 and max. A second reading rides the dropped counts:
the exposures to loss themselves fall with $K$ (fewer re-emissions,
fewer lottery tickets).

** The oscillation, and why Kubernetes hides it

A single spurious object is inserted out of band at round 1 and reported to
the loop over the watch (dead time $dt=2$); the figure traces the net weight
the plant holds for it. The (plant, loop) configurations:

- *additive plant + Reconciler*: the plant integrates deltas with no
  idempotence (an append-only counter, a naive accumulator). Each
  re-emission of the correction stacks; with $k\ge 2$ the loop is unstable
  and the weight rings with growing amplitude - the $z^2=z-1$ pathology the
  control-plane theory warns about, reproduced.
- *idempotent plant + Reconciler*: the plant applies with read-modify-write
  idempotence (a set clamp, $\mathrm{dist}$). The re-emissions are absorbed;
  the weight never leaves $\{0,1\}$ and heals in one dead time. *No
  oscillation - only wasted applies.*
- *idempotent plant + dual-rate Smith*: the disturbance is corrected once
  and stays corrected.

This is the answer to a question the E5 macrobenchmark raised: real
Kubernetes controllers run with feedback dead times well above one step,
yet the marginal oscillation never appears in practice. The reason is not
that the loop is stable - it is that *apiserver apply is idempotent*. A
server-side apply is a $\mathrm{dist}$: re-applying the same object is a
no-op, so the re-emissions that would ring an additive plant are silently
absorbed into waste instead. Kubernetes trades the instability for the
actuation amplification of the first figure - and it is exactly that
amplification the window removes without needing the plant to be
idempotent.

* Reading the three figures together

- *Amplification (fig. 1-2)*: the loop pays redundant applies for the
  dead time its window does not cover - the Reconciler ($K = 0$) pays
  the full $O(dt)$, a window of the dead time pays $O(1)$. On a real apiserver every redundant apply is a request, a
  mutation, a watch event - the amplification is the mechanism behind
  the reconciler echo storms E5 measures, and the sweep prices every
  intermediate window.
- *Stability (fig. 3)*: the redundant applies are harmless only because
  the plant is idempotent. Where the plant is not (an external API
  without server-side apply, a counter, a queue), the same re-emission
  is an instability. The compensated loop is only stated for idempotent
  plants: its internal $\mathrm{dist}$ is the model of exactly that
  plant, so the non-idempotent case contradicts the loop's own model
  and is out of scope.
